# Handoff Brief: Jaclyn Mathai PLJ Final Presentation

## Project objective

Build a visually stunning, emotionally resonant, interactive presentation for Jaclyn Mathai’s final Personal Leadership Journey presentation in her Executive MBA program. The talk is approximately 10 minutes and should reflect on:

- her WHY statement
- her Personal Leadership Development Plan
- her key lessons learned from the PLJ and the last year of leadership growth

This should not feel like a corporate deck, résumé walkthrough, or recitation of a framework. It should feel like a crafted story experience: cinematic, elegant, intimate, intelligent, dynamic, and unmistakably personal.

The presentation should reflect Jaclyn’s voice and identity: interdisciplinary, thoughtful, visually creative, emotionally intelligent, grounded, and strategic. It should feel like art + technology + story + leadership.

Her broader brand already points in this direction; her personal site is branded around “Creative Data, Art & Strategy.”

## Core identity of the speaker

Jaclyn is a senior data science leader, builder, teacher, storyteller, and multidisciplinary creator. Professionally, she has spent the last decade turning complex data, machine learning, and ambiguity into usable products, clear narratives, and strategic decisions. Her work repeatedly sits at the intersection of technical rigor, product thinking, creativity, and human-centered design.

She is not “just” a technical operator, “just” a creative, or “just” a people leader. The most important thing to understand about her is that she lives and leads at intersections:

- art and science
- creativity and analytics
- empathy and accountability
- humanity and AI
- rigor and imagination
- personal history and future-building

This integrated identity is the center of the talk.

## Her current purpose / WHY

Jaclyn’s purpose statement in this PLJ context is:

**Purpose:** blending art and technology to create or amplify stories affirming a sense of belonging.

**Why:** to foster creativity and human connection so people feel a true sense of belonging.

This is not abstract branding language. It comes directly from her life experience.

## Core thesis of the presentation

The strongest narrative framing is:

**This year did not invent Jaclyn’s WHY. It tested it.**

Or, phrased another way:

**Her leadership journey has been about integrating the parts of herself she once thought were separate, and learning that her edge comes not from becoming someone else, but from leading more fully as herself.**

The presentation should show that:

- her WHY is biographical, not generic
- her leadership development is about operationalizing that WHY
- this past year forced her to lead with more clarity, courage, and boundaries
- AI disruption made her WHY more urgent, not less
- she wants to help shape a future where technology expands human creativity, judgment, and belonging rather than diminishing them

## What changed over the last year+

The past year has been one of the hardest of her career. Relevant context:

- She has faced major workplace disruption tied to AI, changing org structures, and new ways of working.
- She has experienced discouragement, insecurity, and low morale after a hard-fought promotion cycle that left her feeling undervalued.
- She has had to navigate difficult leadership dynamics with her boss and SVP.
- She has had to hold a challenging direct report accountable and learn that kindness without clarity is insufficient.
- She has grappled with cynicism and fear around AI, especially in the context of layoffs happening close to home.
- At the same time, she has seen that AI and tools like Claude can increase her ability to build, lead, prototype, learn quickly, and set human-centered vision and guardrails.
- She has realized she does not actually want to abandon data science and technology; she belongs there, especially in work that blends storytelling, product-building, analytics, and human-centered AI.
- While exploring external roles, she rediscovered that there is still something meaningful that draws her to story-driven, creative, high-impact work environments, including Disney.
- She is increasingly drawn to a public identity and leadership brand around human-centered analytics, judgment, allyship, creativity, and responsible AI.

This needs to be framed as a year of disillusionment and recommitment.

## The deepest recurring themes in her life story

These are the big thematic pillars that repeat across her WHY stories and broader journey.

### 1. Belonging

Belonging is not just a nice concept for her. It is central to her biography. She grew up as a half-Korean, half-white farmer’s daughter in a small rural California town where she did not often see people who looked like her and where she experienced othering and racism. Later, she found belonging in more diverse and intellectually expansive communities, especially at Berkeley. She came to understand that she thrives not in rooms where everyone is the same, but in spaces that celebrate the “multi” of human experience.

### 2. Storytelling

She has loved stories since childhood. Reading, writing, staging elaborate plays with her sisters, and later teaching and designing learning experiences were not side hobbies. They are foundational. She sees art as storytelling and believes stories hold power, open minds, and touch the soul.

### 3. Grit and embodiment

Her scoliosis and spinal fusion surgery are central formative experiences. She wore a brace for years, eventually underwent a nine-hour spinal surgery, woke up taller, had to relearn basic independence, and developed a deep gratitude for movement and a relationship to fragility, strength, flexibility, and limits. This story is not merely about resilience. It is also about humility, embodiment, endurance, and why humane systems matter.

### 4. Heritage and carrying stories forward

Her grandparents were born in North Korea and met in Seoul. Her grandfather lost his parents across the border after the Korean War. She exchanged dozens of letters with him and feels a responsibility to carry the stories of her ancestors who survived war, occupation, and immigration. She sees those stories as living in her blood.

### 5. Hard work and cultivation

Her parents left finance to become farmers in rural Central California. Her father learned farming from scratch and built up his land through his own manual labor. She grew up participating in the work, from picking up trash on Christmas Eve to helping with grapes, lemon trees, and pruning almonds. This gave her a visceral respect for labor, craft, patience, and the satisfaction of shaping something tangible.

### 6. Integration of opposites

Her PLDP explicitly names integration of opposites, yin and yang, storytelling, grit, belonging, the body as a record of history, and creating new frontiers in technology while contributing to “magic.” This is not decorative language. This is probably the cleanest abstract description of her identity.

## Motion and interactivity guidance

This should be interactive in service of emotion and narrative, not just novelty.

### Good motion behaviors

- scroll-based reveals that feel like uncovering memory
- parallax layering for depth
- curves that guide the eye like a spine or path
- type appearing with restraint, not flashy kinetic text overload
- image masks and transitions that feel organic
- motion tied to breath, growth, turning pages, unfolding letters, or entering stages/worlds
- subtle use of sound-inspired visual rhythm even if no actual sound is used

### Strong interactive ideas

- a landing sequence where a single line evolves into a spine, road, branch, or timeline
- hover states that reveal story fragments or images
- a branching but guided narrative where the viewer can explore some themes but still follow a clear main path
- transitions between “self,” “history,” “work,” and “future” that feel like merging layers rather than slide changes
- subtle use of sound-inspired visual rhythm even if no actual sound is used

### What to avoid

- over-engineered scrolling gimmicks
- 3D spectacle without narrative purpose
- random particle effects
- excessive text animation
- heavy reliance on stock templates
- anything that feels like a flashy startup product launch page

## Recommended structure for the actual experience

One strong structure would be:

1. **Section 1: Opening / the line** — A minimal but evocative opening. A line appears. It could be a spine, field row, path, branch, or brushstroke. Voice/theme: this may not look like leadership, but this is where mine started.

2. **Section 2: The stories that shaped me** — Introduce 2–3 foundational story pillars: scoliosis / body / grit; farm / labor / cultivation; belonging / identity / finding the multi. Optional: letters / ancestry.

3. **Section 3: My WHY** — Bring the stories together into the purpose statement: blending art and technology to amplify stories affirming belonging.

4. **Section 4: The year that tested it** — Show the tension: AI disruption; layoffs and fear; political leadership dynamics; hard accountability moments; insecurity about fit and future.

5. **Section 5: What I learned about leadership** — Land the lessons: clarity is kindness; humane culture requires boundaries; AI increases the need for judgment; I do belong here; my edge is integration.

6. **Section 6: Future-facing close** — Recommitment: I want to build tools, teams, and futures where people have more room to imagine, create, and belong.
