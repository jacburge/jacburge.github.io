# PLJ visual storyboard & build brief

This document is the **source of truth** for the `/emba-plj` experience: a **digital art film** with live narration—not a slide deck.

---

## Two worlds (felt, never labeled on screen)

**World A — Organic / inherited / embodied / tactile**  
Farm, body, ancestry, letters, roots, brushstrokes, paper texture, warmth, memory.

**World B — Technical / future-facing / luminous / structured**  
Data, AI, systems, interfaces, networks, geometry, light, precision, possibility.

The audience should **feel** the contrast and the **gradual merge**, not read an explanation.

---

## System logic (non-negotiables)

- One **recurring line motif** across all scenes: spine → branch → furrow → fold → brushstroke → waveform → path → unified line.
- Same **palette family** and **texture family** throughout; each scene is a **variation**, not a new random style.
- **No** bullet points, standard slide titles, stock layouts, corporate tech gradients, cliché AI iconography, dense text, or literal story-on-screen.
- **Sparse optional text only** (phrases below); generous negative space; **cinematic, editorial, museum-installation** energy.

## Cursor / builder prompt (paste for future iterations)

Build a visually stunning interactive presentation for a 10-minute live personal leadership talk. This is not a traditional slide deck. The visuals should not explicitly tell the story or summarize the speaker’s points. The speaker will tell the story live in her own words. The presentation should function as poetic visual storytelling that creates mood, metaphor, contrast, transformation, and emotional amplification.

Core concept: two visual worlds gradually merge over the course of the presentation. One world is organic, tactile, embodied, archival, earthy, rooted in memory, ancestry, farming, paper, brushstrokes, handwritten letters, roots, branches, and physical history. The other world is luminous, geometric, technological, data-inspired, future-facing, precise, and structured, rooted in analytics, AI, systems, networks, grids, and light. The audience should feel these contrasting worlds gradually integrating until they become one unified visual language.

Use one recurring line motif throughout the experience. This line should transform across scenes into a spine curve, branch, orchard row, paper fold, brushstroke, waveform, path, and network connection. This recurring line is the backbone of the entire visual system.

Create 10 scenes, each with a distinct emotional role but consistent art direction:

1. opening tension between two worlds  
2. body / grit / memory  
3. farm / labor / cultivation  
4. letters / ancestry / inheritance  
5. belonging / fragmentation / multiplicity  
6. creativity / storytelling / imagination  
7. present-day collision with AI, leadership pressure, and uncertainty  
8. integration and realization  
9. clarity, boundaries, humane leadership  
10. future-facing horizon / open path  

Design requirements:

- sparse text only, if any; never use bullet points  
- no generic slide titles  
- no standard corporate deck layouts  
- no stock imagery feel  
- no cliché AI graphics  
- use negative space generously  
- use editorial typography and cinematic pacing  
- incorporate layered textures, translucency, split-screen compositions that later dissolve, masking, subtle parallax, and elegant motion transitions  
- visuals should feel like a premium digital art film or museum installation adapted for live presentation  
- the screen must support spoken narration, not compete with it  
- use restrained but meaningful interactivity or click-triggered transitions  
- prioritize beauty, cohesion, symbolism, and transformation over information density  

Aesthetic direction:

- sophisticated, emotional, modern, artful, not corporate  
- palette of deep ink, warm earth, muted gold, orchard green, parchment, and restrained luminous highlights  
- combine tactile materials like paper, ink, grain, botanical texture, and handwriting atmosphere with precise light, data contours, networks, and geometry  
- motion should be slow, intentional, and emotionally timed, with 1–2 major jaw-dropping transformation moments when the two worlds merge  

Important: do not make a deck that “explains” the story. Build a visual environment that mirrors the speaker’s inner landscape and thematic arc.

Do not design this as slides with content. Design it as a sequence of immersive visual tableaux with minimal text. Each scene should feel like a moving piece of visual poetry. The audience should be able to watch the screen and feel the emotional arc even if they are not reading anything. Prioritize metaphor, transformation, composition, and motion over explicit explanation.
